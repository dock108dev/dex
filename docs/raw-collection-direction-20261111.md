# Raw Pokémon collecting: direction and impact audit

Decision recorded November 11, 2026. This is a planning and source-code audit, not an implementation or a claim about currently installed behavior. It updates the priorities of the [Desktop execution tracker](../../dex_next_steps.md); historical delivery records remain evidence of what was previously built and verified.

## Product direction

Dex's first job is to help someone build a raw Pokémon collection and Pokédex: understand what they own and are missing, find relevant raw cards or lots, and judge asking prices using transparent, applicable ungraded evidence. Collecting is the goal; profit is not required. Graded-card support, resale-return optimization, sports cards and other categories are later opportunities, not pre-beta requirements. The owner's view of collector preferences is product direction, not a measured statistic about all collectors.

**Preserve graded functionality and data.** Introduce a centrally controlled, default-off graded-experience feature flag in implementation. With it off, remove graded scenarios, graded inputs and slab-focused choices from ordinary product journeys, including entry points, results, details, saved flows and collection editing. Do not delete the logic, stored grade, grading company, certificate, import/export information or historical research. Never silently rewrite a graded copy as raw, remove ownership, or overwrite hidden fields when editing another field. Existing graded records must remain truthful and safely identifiable; design a concise read-only indication or equivalent preservation path rather than hiding a material fact. The flag is not a security boundary or a reason to expose data to another account. An enabled mode must remain recoverable and testable. Pick the concrete configuration owner and migration-free default after auditing existing settings; a capability marker alone is not assumed to be a feature flag.

Raw shopping should seek ungraded listings, but provider labels and seller descriptions are claims, not proof. Clearly graded/slabbed items should not be treated as raw matches; uncertain cases must be labelled or placed separately rather than silently classified as ungraded. Lots may contain unknown or mixed contents: keep them visible with uncertainty rather than assert that every card is raw. Never reinterpret a graded reference as raw.

This is a cross-cutting **medium-to-large product/UX change**: much of the underlying collection, listing and evidence infrastructure is reusable, but a complete flag, saved-state compatibility, listing classification and raw-price source qualification span multiple layers. An exact estimate and complete surface inventory require the first implementation audit. Qualified condition-matched sold coverage may be the largest external dependency; this document does not establish that a suitable source exists or is available.

## Price comparison contract

For singles, show the exact supported card identity, seller's stated condition and its uncertainty, asking price, shipping and any known/unknown taxes or other charges. Compare like-for-like raw observations only when source rights, identity/printing, currency, condition and money basis support the comparison. Distinguish observed sales from listings and guides; display source, observation period/date, sample and spread when available. Never describe a mixed-condition publisher guide as condition-matched sold data, or equate item-only sales with a delivered offer without explaining the difference. Do not manufacture condition or grade prices. No defensible match means useful listing facts plus a plain unavailable reason, not an invented deal or a false precision score. Auction bids are provisional.

For lots, show supported collection additions, quantities, unresolved contents and costs; reference only independently supported raw cards and eligible evidence. An identified-card subtotal is not a complete lot value. Avoid counting duplicate mentions or pretending condition, shipping or missing cards are known. Retain source expiry, revocation, capture limits and the separate native guide semantics in the existing contracts.

## Ordered work after this audit

1. **RAW-01 — Inventory and UX design.** Trace every customer-facing grade/slab reference, route, API and saved/import/edit path, listing classifier, reference selector and navigation item. Inspect the actual configured customer runtime and current coverage read-only; record which findings are source-only versus installed observations. Map existing graded copies and research safely without exposing private contents. Specify a central flag, default-off behavior, server/UI propagation and re-enable/rollback contract. Design collection → missing card → raw singles, plus mixed lots and clear empty/unknown states for desktop, narrow screens and keyboard use. Resolve interaction details as engineering decisions within the owner's direction.
2. **RAW-02 — Flag and preservation.** Implement the flag and off-mode across the audited surfaces without deleting graded code or mutating retained data. Enforce truthful server-side selection as well as UI hiding. Verify off/on transitions, existing graded records, edits with hidden fields, saved research, imports/exports, direct routes, permissions and source expiry. Ensure ordinary raw edits never erase graded attributes or turn a slab into a raw reference.
3. **RAW-03 — Raw collecting and shopping UX.** Make the collection/index and missing-card shopping path the primary experience. Apply honest raw/graded/unknown listing treatment; keep mixed lots and user corrections usable. Remove or demote resale-first prompts and grade-oriented language from ordinary flows, while preserving underlying functionality. Test results, details, Back/reload, empty/partial/no-evidence and accessibility, including narrow screens.
4. **RAW-04 — Price evidence.** Recheck current exact-printing coverage and independently investigate permitted, affordable raw sold/comparable sources and their contracts, condition and shipping semantics, refresh/retention and mappings. Use finite authorized acquisition only where necessary. Admit and activate qualifying sources under existing rights, expiry and Stop controls. If unavailable, name the precise limitation and keep useful native guides accurately labelled; do not make source unavailability block independent UX work.
5. **RAW-05 — Prove the real journey.** Run focused tests and hosted checks for the exact delivered commit; back up and update the existing installation with rollback as documented. Verify on real customer data: owned/missing Pokémon → exact card → raw singles → offer and applicable price evidence; also a mixed lot and an existing graded record where available. Verify off/on preservation and unavailable evidence with appropriate engineering tests. Record real source coverage, dates, costs and observations separately from fixture results, installed behavior and owner acceptance. Ask for owner review only after agreed engineering is complete, or report a concrete external blocker and its precise impact.

## Acceptance examples

- A clearly slabbed single is not presented as an ungraded match; an ambiguous offer remains uncertain. A mixed lot is not automatically excluded merely because each card's state is unknown.
- A graded copy already in the collection still contributes to owned/index progress. Editing a binder or condition while the flag is off preserves its grader, grade and certificate; enabling the flag reveals the same retained data.
- A raw listing with matching condition-scoped sold evidence shows the comparison's source, dates, sample and cost basis. With only a mixed-condition guide, it is labelled accordingly and no condition-specific saving is asserted. With no eligible evidence, the listing remains useful and the gap is explicit.
- No graded scenario or graded input appears in ordinary off-mode navigation, details, calculations, saved flows or accessible labels; enabling the flag restores supported functionality without altering customer data.
- Completion of this direction does not require a 10% resale opportunity, a graded-card valuation, a provider purchase, or an unrelated external deployment.
